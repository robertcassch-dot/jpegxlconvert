Privacy & Cookie Policy

Last updated: 15 October 2026

1. The short version

Your images and files are never uploaded. The converter and the viewer both run inside your own browser, using WebAssembly. The files you open here stay on your device. They are not sent to us, and there is nowhere on this site that could receive them.

There is no sign-up, no account, no contact form and no newsletter. We hold no database about you.

One thing on this site can identify you: Google's advertising system, which is what pays for the site. Section 6 explains exactly what it does, and section 8 explains the consent message you are shown in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, and how to say no from it. There is also anonymous audience measurement, which sets no cookie and no identifier — section 5.

2. Who is responsible for your data

This website (JXL Convert, jpegxlconvert.com) is run by one private individual, in a personal capacity. There is no company behind it, and none is involved in it.

Data controller: that same private individual. The way to reach them, for anything in this policy and to exercise the rights in section 12, is contact@jpegxlconvert.com. It is the same address in all five language versions.

There is no Data Protection Officer, because none of the situations listed in Article 37 GDPR applies here.

3. How the converter works, and why we never receive your files

This is the part that makes everything else in this policy true, so here is the mechanism in plain words.

  • When you pick a file, your browser reads it straight off your own disk using the File API. Nothing is sent anywhere at that point.
  • The decoding and re-encoding is done by libjxl and libheif, compiled to WebAssembly. Those codecs are served from this same domain (/assets/vendor/) and then run in your computer's memory.
  • The converted image is handed back to you as a local download, from a blob your browser creates. It never travels.

There is no upload endpoint anywhere on this site. The direct consequence is that your files cannot appear in the hosting logs, because they never leave your machine to begin with.

You do not have to take our word for it. Two things you can check yourself:

  • Open the Network tab of your browser's developer tools and convert a file. You will see the codec being loaded from this domain, and no request carrying your image.
  • Convert one image, then disconnect from the internet and convert another. It still works: the codecs are already in your browser's cache and no further request is made. On a first visit the codec itself does have to be downloaded from this domain first, which is why the order matters — that download is the only thing on this page that needs the network.

The two files that drive the converter and the viewer (assets/js/app.js and assets/js/viewer.js) contain not one network call: no fetch, no XMLHttpRequest, no WebSocket, no sendBeacon.

4. What we do not process

  • The images and files you convert or view. Their content never reaches us.
  • Their metadata and EXIF data. The tool reads the EXIF orientation locally so it does not rotate your photo the wrong way, and that is where it ends: nothing is sent anywhere.
  • Account data. There is no sign-up, no login and no password.
  • Your name, address or phone number.
  • Payment data. The tool is free.
  • There is no contact form and no newsletter.
  • This site sets no cookies of its own. The only thing that can be stored under this domain is the record of your own answer to the consent message, and that is written by Google's consent platform, not by our code — section 8.

8. Your consent (EEA, UK and Switzerland)

If you arrive from the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, this site shows you a consent message before Google may personalise the advertising. It is run by Google's own consent management platform (CMP), which is certified by Google and integrated with the IAB's Transparency and Consent Framework (TCF). Google has required a certified, TCF-integrated CMP for serving ads in the EEA and the UK since 16 January 2024, and in Switzerland since 31 July 2024: without one, traffic from those countries is not eligible for personalised ads at all.

The three options the message gives you, in its own words:

  • Consent — Google and the vendors listed in the message may store and read advertising identifiers on your device and use them to personalise the ads you are shown.
  • Do not consent — refusing, in one click, on the same screen. It is a button sitting beside the other one, not something buried in a submenu, so saying no costs exactly the effort of saying yes. No dark patterns.
  • Manage options — the detailed second layer, where you decide purpose by purpose and vendor by vendor, see who each vendor is, and read how your choices are stored on your device.

Refusing does not take the ads away — it takes the profiling away. You still see advertising, chosen from the content of the page and a rough location instead of from a profile built about you. Non-personalised ads still need an identifier of some kind, to cap how often you see the same ad and to detect click fraud; that part does not run on your consent, and Google relies on legitimate interest for it (section 5).

Refusing does not break the converter either. The tool behaves identically with advertising refused. It has to: the conversion runs entirely in your browser and has nothing to do with the ads. Ad blockers remain expressly allowed by our Terms of Use.

What the message stores, and where. Your answer is written to your own device as a TCF consent string — either in a cookie or in your browser's local storage, whichever the consent platform uses; the TCF specification leaves that choice to it. The string is scoped to this domain: it applies here and is not shared with other websites, TCF's old global scope having been withdrawn in September 2021. What it holds is the decision plus the context needed to read it back: the TCF version, when it was created and last updated, the identity and version of the consent platform, the screen and language you were shown, your consent for each purpose, the legitimate-interest signals, your consent for each individual vendor, the restrictions set by this site, and the list of vendors that were presented to you. There is no name in it, no email address, and nothing you typed into the converter.

That record exists so your decision can be honoured and evidenced — Google's EU User Consent Policy requires naming every party that may receive your data, obtaining consent both for storage on your device and for ad personalisation, keeping a record of that consent, and giving clear instructions for taking it back — and so that you are not asked again on every page: once you have answered, the message stays away unless it is updated or consent has to be collected afresh. How long the record lasts is declared by the message itself, in the "Manage options" layer, which is where the TCF rules place that disclosure. We do not repeat a figure here that we cannot verify ourselves.

How to change your mind. The footer of every page carries a "Privacy settings" link. It clears the answer stored on your device and reopens the message with the same three options, so withdrawing is as easy as giving — which is exactly what Article 7(3) GDPR requires. Withdrawing does not make what happened before it unlawful; it stops the processing from that moment on. AdSense also adds a revocation link of its own to sites that show this message, so you may find two routes to the same screen.

Two honest details about that link. It appears once Google's consent code has confirmed that these rules apply to you; if you are browsing from outside the EEA, the UK and Switzerland there is no message to reopen, and the link stays hidden. And if it is missing where you expect it, the usual cause is an ad blocker or a privacy extension blocking Google's consent code — in which case the message it would reopen has not run either. Your browser is the fallback: clearing this site's data deletes the stored answer, and the message returns on your next visit.

Ways to say no that do not depend on the message at all, wherever you are: My Ad Center, blocking third-party cookies in your browser settings, and ad blockers.
