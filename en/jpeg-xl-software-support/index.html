Which Apps Support JPEG XL?

Support has grown steadily since the format was standardised, and October 2026 changed the browser picture: Chrome and Firefox now show JPEG XL by default. It's still uneven in ways that surprise people — your phone may open a .jxl that your Windows PC can't. Here's the state of play, without the cheerleading.

by Robert Castillo · Published August 3, 2026 · updated October 15, 2026

Operating systems

Apple is the outlier, in a good way. JPEG XL has been part of the system since September 2023, with iOS and iPadOS 17 and macOS Sonoma 14: on iPhone and iPad it opens in Safari, Files and Photos, and on the Mac in Safari, Preview and Quick Look, with Finder thumbnails too. Preview only joined in after Sonoma's early updates. If you're on a recent Mac or iPhone, the format effectively already won.

Windows can open .jxl, but only with help and only on recent versions. Microsoft's free JPEG XL Image Extension in the Microsoft Store adds thumbnails to File Explorer and lets the Photos app open the files — but it installs only on Windows 11 24H2 or later. On the 24H2 machine we tested it wasn't there out of the box, and without it Windows couldn't decode a .jxl. On Windows 10 and older Windows 11 releases there's no Microsoft option at all, so a third-party viewer is the way in. Our Windows guide walks through both routes.

Linux depends on your desktop. GNOME and KDE image viewers have gained support through their imaging libraries, and anything built on ImageMagick, libvips or GdkPixbuf with the right plugin will handle it. On a current distribution it usually works; on an older one it usually doesn't.

October 2026 changed this table more than the previous three years did. Safari carried JPEG XL almost alone from 2023; then Chrome 155 switched it on by default on 6 October and Firefox 158 followed on 13 October. The details, including what to do on older versions, are in the Chrome guide and the Firefox guide, and our browser test tells you in a second whether yours can show it.

The big exception is Microsoft Edge. It's built on Chromium, yet Edge 155 doesn't display JPEG XL and has no flag for it: Microsoft removed it from the Edge 155 release notes on 9 October 2026, and in our own test Edge simply downloaded a .jxl instead of showing it. This site works differently, which is why it runs in Edge too: the decoder is compiled to WebAssembly and shipped with the page, so it doesn't need the browser to support the format at all.

Photo editors and professional tools

🎨

Photoshop and Lightroom

Photoshop opens, edits and saves JPEG XL natively since version 26.8 (June 2025) — look under File › Save a Copy, not Export As. Camera Raw has read and written it since version 16.0 in 2023, and Lightroom Classic 13 exports it and uses it inside lossy DNGs. Details in the Photoshop guide and the Lightroom guide.

🖼️

Affinity Photo

Affinity Photo 2 opens and exports JPEG XL, according to Serif's own file-format table.

🐧

GIMP

Supports JPEG XL import and export in current versions through libjxl. If yours doesn't, you're on an older build — this is one of the cases where updating actually fixes it. Our GIMP guide explains the export dialog.

📷

darktable and RawTherapee

darktable exports JPEG XL, which matters for photographers who want an archival master that isn't a 100 MB TIFF. RawTherapee only opens .jxl files so far (since 5.11); export is planned for version 6.0.

🔍

XnView MP and IrfanView

Two free viewers that handle it on Windows, and both batch-convert. XnView MP, free for private use, reads JPEG XL as it comes. IrfanView, free for non-commercial use, needs its separate PlugIns pack, whose JPEG_XL plugin reads and writes the format. On Windows 10, or any Windows without Microsoft's extension, start here.

⚙️

ImageMagick and libvips

libvips packages usually include JPEG XL; ImageMagick depends on the build — Homebrew's, for one, is built without it, so check yours with magick -list format before you script around it. For scripted or server-side processing this is where the real work gets done.

The reference tools

Underneath nearly everything above sits libjxl, the open-source reference implementation, and its two command-line tools: cjxl to encode and djxl to decode. They are the most complete and up-to-date implementations that exist, and they're free.

For anyone processing images in bulk they're also simply the right tool — a GUI is a poor way to convert forty thousand files. cjxl photo.jpg photo.jxl losslessly re-packs an existing JPEG, and djxl reverses it exactly.

If that's the direction you're heading, the practical walkthrough is in archiving a photo library without losing quality.

One thing none of the browsers above can do is create JPEG XL. Chrome, Firefox and Safari decode the format but don't encode it, so making .jxl files still means one of these tools, an editor from the list above or a converter.

What this means in practice

If you just received one file

Install nothing. On a computer with Chrome 155+, Firefox 158+ or Safari, drag it into a tab; anywhere else, phones included, open it here or convert it to JPG and get on with your day. Installing a codec to look at one photo is a poor trade.

If you're on Windows and see them regularly

On Windows 11 24H2 or later, add Microsoft's free JPEG XL Image Extension from the Store first: it gives you thumbnails in File Explorer and opening in Photos. On Windows 10 or an older Windows 11, install XnView MP, or IrfanView together with its PlugIns pack, instead. The Windows guide has the steps for each.

If you're on a Mac

With macOS Sonoma 14 or later you already have what you need: double-click the file and Preview opens it, Quick Look previews it and Finder shows thumbnails. On Apple devices, iPhone and iPad included, JPEG XL behaves like a normal format.

If you're publishing images on the web

JPEG XL is now usable on the web — Chrome 155+, Firefox 158+ and Safari 17+ show it — but not as the only format. Edge 155 can't display it, Samsung Internet is unconfirmed and many visitors haven't updated. Serve it inside a <picture> with AVIF, WebP or JPEG behind it, and read the AVIF comparison before choosing.

FAQ

Why doesn't Windows Photos open .jxl?
Because the codec isn't installed. Microsoft's free JPEG XL Image Extension adds it — once installed, Photos opens .jxl files and File Explorer shows thumbnails — but the extension only installs on Windows 11 24H2 or later. On Windows 10 or an older Windows 11, Photos can't help; use a viewer such as XnView MP or IrfanView with its PlugIns pack, or our browser viewer.
Do I need a plugin to open .jxl in Photoshop?
Not any more. Photoshop 26.8, the June 2025 release, opens, edits and saves JPEG XL itself, through File › Save a Copy. For older versions on Windows, from Photoshop 2020 onwards, a community plugin built on libjxl, JpegXlFormat, is the usual route. Our Photoshop guide covers the save options.
Does Chrome support JPEG XL now?
Yes. Chrome 155, released on 6 October 2026, shows JPEG XL by default on the desktop and, on Android, in web pages — more than three years after Chrome 110 removed it. Chrome 145 to 154 had it behind a flag called "Enable JXL image format". The full story is here.
What's the safest format to send someone?
JPEG, still, and it isn't close. Every device made in the last thirty years opens it. Use JPEG XL for what you keep and JPEG for what you send.
Can Android open .jxl files?
In web pages, yes now: Chrome 155 and Firefox 158 show JPEG XL images on websites. A .jxl saved on the phone is another story. Chrome won't open it, Android itself has no JPEG XL decoder, and we found no phone maker documenting .jxl support in its own gallery, so plan as if yours doesn't. Our Android guide lists the apps that bring their own decoder.
Do I need to install anything to use this site?
No. The encoder and decoder are compiled to WebAssembly and load with the page, which is why it works even in browsers that can't display JPEG XL themselves, such as Edge 155. Your files stay on your device throughout.
