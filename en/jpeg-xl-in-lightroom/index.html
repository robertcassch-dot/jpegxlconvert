JPEG XL in Lightroom

Lightroom Classic has exported and imported .jxl since version 13.0 (October 2023), and the cloud-based Lightroom since 7.0. The export option sits in the same File Settings panel as JPEG. The part most people miss is elsewhere: since 13.0, Lightroom's lossy DNGs are compressed with JPEG XL too.

The short answer

Yes, both Lightrooms do JPEG XL. Lightroom Classic 13.0, released on 10 October 2023, added JPEG XL and AVIF to the export formats alongside its new HDR editing, and Adobe's HDR guide says that with HDR Output on, Classic 13 and later can open and save photos in AVIF, JPEG or JPEG XL. The cloud-based Lightroom 7.0 arrived the same month with the same formats.

Importing works as well: Adobe's list of supported formats includes JPEG XL for both apps. In October 2026 the current Lightroom Classic is 15.6, and none of the release notes since 14.4 mention JPEG XL — the feature has been stable for a while.

The less visible change came in the same 13.0 release: lossy DNGs switched from JPEG to JPEG XL compression, and so did the DNGs Lightroom creates itself with Merge to HDR and Panorama — and, until Classic 14.4 (June 2025), the DNGs that Enhance wrote; since 14.4 those enhancements apply without a separate DNG. That is why some of your DNGs suddenly got much smaller — and why other programs may have stopped previewing them.

How to export JPEG XL from Lightroom Classic

Open the Export dialog

Select the photos and choose File › Export (Ctrl+Shift+E on Windows, Cmd+Shift+E on a Mac). Set Export To: Hard Drive and pick the folder as usual.

Choose the format

In File Settings, open Image Format and pick JPEG XL. It sits in the same list as JPEG, AVIF, PSD, TIFF, PNG, DNG and Original.

Set quality, colour and depth

Use the Quality slider (0–100) or tick the Lossless option; then pick the Color Space and a Bit Depth of 8 or 16 bits per component.

HDR only if you edited in HDR

Tick HDR Output only for photos edited in HDR mode; add Maximize Compatibility to embed a gain map so non-HDR screens still get a proper image.

Finish as for a JPEG

Image Sizing, Output Sharpening, Metadata and Watermarking all work with JPEG XL. Click Add under the presets panel to save the settings, then Export.

One thing JPEG XL doesn't get is Limit File Size To, which Adobe documents for JPEG only. If you need exports under a fixed size, either stay with JPEG for that preset or find the Quality value that lands under your limit with a test batch.

The colour-space advice isn't fussiness. Since June 2024 users have reported coloured block artefacts in lossy JPEG XL exports in ProPhoto RGB and Adobe RGB — the first report came from Classic 13.3.1, and it was still being seen on 14.1.1 in January 2025. The workaround suggested in Adobe's forum is simple: sRGB, Display P3 or Rec. 2020 for lossy exports, and Lossless when you really need Adobe RGB or ProPhoto.

Measured: Quality, Lossless and 16-bit in megabytes

Lightroom doesn't say how its Quality slider maps onto the JPEG XL encoder, so we measured the encoder itself: libjxl 0.12.0, the reference implementation, on two public-domain photos (14 and 45 megapixels). Quality is scored with SSIMULACRA2, where 80 means no difference visible side by side at 1:1 and 90 means visually lossless. This approximates Lightroom's choices with libjxl; it isn't Lightroom's own output.

Three things stand out. First, on photo A, JPEG XL at distance 1.0 is a third smaller than a quality-90 JPEG and scores higher; at distance 2.0 it is a third of the JPEG's size for a score only two points lower. That is the gain you are exporting JPEG XL for. Photo B, a 45-megapixel portrait full of fabric and fine detail, is a harder test: there distance 1.0 came out 41% smaller than the quality-90 JPEG (2.91 against 4.92 MB) but scored a little lower, 77.2 against 78.6. Distance 0.75, about quality 93, beat the JPEG on both counts with 3.79 MB and 79.8, so detailed photos deserve a slightly higher setting.

Second, 16-bit costs almost nothing with lossy compression: 990,980 bytes in 8-bit, 991,004 in 16-bit. The encoder works in floating point internally, so the extra precision is nearly free. Photo B told the same story: 2,914,782 bytes in 8-bit, 2,914,774 in 16-bit.

Third, the 16-bit Lossless row is misleading for raw files. Our sources are 8-bit JPEGs, so the extra bits carry no information and the file grew only 1.3% (1.6% on photo B). Export a 16-bit Lossless file from a real raw and the low bits are full of genuine detail and noise; expect sizes in TIFF territory. Lossless 16-bit is for masters, not for sharing.

Lightroom (the cloud version)

The cloud-based Lightroom got JPEG XL in version 7.0, in the same October 2023 release that brought HDR editing. Adobe's help lists JPG, AVIF, JPEG XL, DNG, TIF and Original as export types. Choose Export › Custom Settings, pick the image type and, for photos edited in HDR, tick HDR Output.

The cloud app's export screen is simpler than Classic's, and Adobe's help doesn't document separate JPEG XL controls such as a Lossless box or bit depth there. If you need those, export from Classic, or export a 16-bit TIFF and convert it afterwards.

Both apps import .jxl files into the library. Early on, Classic 13.0 had a known issue where JPEG XL and AVIF files couldn't be picked out by file type in smart collections or the Metadata panel; it's worth checking that a smart collection catches your .jxl files before you rely on it.

Lossy DNG: the JPEG XL inside your raw files

DNG is Adobe's open raw format, and its 1.7 specification (June 2023, revised as 1.7.1 in September 2023) added JPEG XL as a compression method, with tags to record its distance and effort. Lightroom Classic started using it in 13.0 for lossy DNGs: those you create with Use Lossy Compression when exporting or converting to DNG, as long as Compatibility is set to Camera Raw 15.3 or later; the DNGs that Merge to HDR and Panorama write for you; and, until Classic 14.4 (June 2025), the DNGs that Enhance (Denoise, Super Resolution) wrote. Since 14.4, Denoise, Raw Details and Super Resolution are ordinary Detail-panel edits and no longer create a separate DNG.

The effect on size is large. One user on Classic 13.3 converted a 22 MB raw to lossy DNG: about 5 MB with JPEG XL, against about 10 MB with the older JPEG-based lossy DNG. Greg Benz, who writes widely on Lightroom, cites about 92% smaller with his 50+ MB raw files; what you save depends on the camera and how it compresses its raw. And the Denoise DNGs of that period, which in 12.5 often came out at four times the size of the raw, dropped to roughly twice its size.

What it implies for your raw files: a lossy DNG is no longer a raw in the strict sense. Lightroom's lossy DNGs store an already demosaiced (linear) image instead of the sensor's colour-filter mosaic — the older JPEG-based ones did too — so the camera's original pattern is gone. Since Classic 14.0 Denoise also accepts linear DNGs, lossy ones included, but the original sensor data is gone, so keep the original raw for the photos that matter. Keep lossy DNGs for archives of rejects, second copies or travel libraries, not for the photos that matter most.

There's a compatibility price too. With JPEG XL, even the embedded preview can be JPEG XL, so Windows Explorer shows a generic icon and ordinary viewers can't open the file. darktable lists DNG 1.7 with JPEG XL among the formats it explicitly doesn't support. If a DNG has to open elsewhere, set Compatibility to Camera Raw 14.0 or earlier when exporting it, and accept the larger file.

FAQ

Can Lightroom Classic export JPEG XL?
Yes, since version 13.0 (October 2023). In the Export dialog, choose JPEG XL under File Settings › Image Format. Quality, a Lossless option, colour space, 8- or 16-bit and HDR Output are all available.
What Quality should I use for JPEG XL in Lightroom?
Adobe doesn't publish how the slider maps to the encoder, so test: export one photo at a few values and compare at 100%. One user found that JPEG XL at 95 already came out smaller than his JPEG at 80, while 100 was huge. Don't read our table's quality column as Lightroom's slider: on libjxl's scale, quality 95 weighed 2.03 MB against 0.84 MB for the JPEG at 80. libjxl's quality 90 is a good starting point; on our high-detail photo it took 0.75 (≈93) to beat a quality-90 JPEG.
Can Lightroom import .jxl files?
Yes. Adobe lists JPEG XL among the formats both Lightroom Classic and Lightroom import.
Does Lightroom compress my raw files with JPEG XL?
Only lossy DNGs and the DNGs created by Merge to HDR and Panorama — and, until Classic 14.4 (June 2025), the DNGs that Enhance wrote; since 14.4 those enhancements apply without a separate DNG. Your original raw files and standard (lossless) DNGs are not touched by it.
Should I convert my raw archive to lossy DNG?
Not the photos you care about. A lossy DNG keeps the editing latitude of a demosaiced 16-bit image but loses the original sensor data, and fewer programs can open it. It's a good fit for rejects and duplicate libraries.
Why export JPEG XL instead of JPEG?
Smaller files at the same visual quality, 16-bit and HDR support, and a true lossless mode. The catch is compatibility; see JPEG XL vs JPEG for when it's worth it.
