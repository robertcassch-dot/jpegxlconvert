Which format should you actually use?

Most of the confusion around image formats comes from treating them as rivals when they answer different questions. The short version: JPG is what you send to other people, PNG is what you keep when every pixel matters, and JPEG XL is what you store when you want both — provided whatever opens it supports the format.

HEIC is the odd one out. It is the format an iPhone saves to by default, it compresses beautifully, and it is the single most common reason a photo refuses to open on a Windows machine. If a picture from a relative won't display, HEIC is the first thing to check.

FormatBest forThe catch
JPGSending photos to anyone, anywhereLossy: quality degrades every time you re-save
PNGScreenshots, logos, transparencyMuch larger files than JPG for photographs
JPEG XLStoring and archiving at high qualityChrome, Firefox and Safari show it now; Edge and older software still don’t
HEICSaving space on an iPhoneFrequently unopenable on Windows and the web

If you only remember one thing: converting a file never improves it. Convert to solve a compatibility problem, or to save space losslessly — not in the hope of a better picture.
